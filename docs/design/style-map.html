ThesisA grant is already a rectangle. Draw it as one.

A lumberroom grant is a namespace glob and a sensitivity ceiling. That is a coordinate pair, so the set of facts it admits is an area over a two-axis field. Draw the store as that field and a grant stops being a sentence the owner has to trust and becomes a shape they can see before committing it.

Nothing else in this console needs inventing. The map is the inventory for job 1, the reach overlay is job 2, the same field re-rendered under another client's grant is job 8, and a supersession is a line between two points in one cell.

One structural rule holds the whole thing together: the map is output and never input. Clauses are the control, and the field redraws as the consequence. The specification bans a grant matrix as an editing surface because globs overlap and nest, and that ban is correct. A read-only grid of concrete namespaces is allowed, so the field shows the resolved ceiling per namespace, which is the union rule in domain/policy.rs ceiling(): the most generous matching clause wins, and the clause that won is named when the owner focuses the cell.

Optimised for

Job 2, decide what a surface may see and see the consequence first. Job 8, what one client would get for one question. Job 1's inventory half, which is the field itself, sitting beside an arrivals rail that carries the other half.

Sacrificed, on purpose

Job 6 gets a list docked under the map instead of a ritual of its own. A triage inbox will beat this at conflicts and should. Job 9 is a strip inside the fact panel, four lines tall, and it earns no more.

The second visit, not the first

A spatial interface that impresses once and dies is the known failure of this style. The counter is in the cells: every cell carries what arrived this week under its total, so the field is different on Monday than it was on Friday. That number comes from created_at through ledger item 1 and needs no visit tracking, which nothing has funded.

Reading the mapSeven rows, three columns, one union.

Rows are namespaces, sorted so that a glob covers a contiguous block: global, then user:me, then every project:, then every personal:, then credentials:. Columns are the sensitivity axis in its own order, open to sealed. A clause is therefore a rectangle, and a grant is the union of its rectangles.

The sort order is the design work. Put personal:health between two project namespaces and project:* stops being a rectangle, which is the failure mode named in the closing panel.

Fact text is capped at 75ch in the stylesheet. There is no comfortable mode; this is the resting state.
2Two of size, weight and family separate each level Fact text is 15px serif at 400. Metadata is 12.5px mono at 400. Chrome is 14px sans at 600. A section label is 11.5px sans at 700, uppercase. Every adjacent pair differs by family and by one more axis, so flattening a screenshot to three tones keeps the levels apart. Lightness carries nothing on its own.
3Client, date and confirmation within 20 characters of the fact, never a ragged column Provenance is inline in the same text flow as the fact, one middot after the last word, so it wraps with the sentence it belongs to. There is no provenance column to go ragged. Cover every row but one and the row still reads as a unit.
4Sensitivity on three channels beyond hue Four. The printed word, the marker shape (hollow, filled, crosshatched), the row or cell texture, and the column the count sits in on the field. A black and white print sorts by any one of them.
5An ungranted cell reads at 4.5:1 or better; the widest grant is not the hardest text no reach measures 14.11:1 and the withheld count beside it 7.68:1. 6Nothing hover-only, every keyboard verb has a visible control, Escape steps out Every verb in every mockup is a labelled button with its key printed on its face. 7No status band outweighs the content beneath it The health line is one line of 13px mono in the top bar and is the same line on all five screens. A warning band is one sentence at 13.5px with 8px of padding. The key mismatch gets a full treatment on one screen, in states frame five.
8Nothing moves by default; held keys transition at zero; a hold gate is timed in JavaScript This file declares no transition and no animation on any product element. Holding j or k moves the selection with nothing to interpolate. There is no hold-to-confirm gate at all: the second act is a discrete press on a control that was not on screen a moment earlier, so there is no timer for a motion preference to shorten.
9No cosine score at rest; bands with printed headers; the third says nothing matched well The lens prints close match, possible and nothing matched well in those words, and the queue prints near-identical wording instead of 0.94. 10No implementation-status chips and no annotation inside the product Search the mockups for wired, not built or a ledger number and you find none. Every marker in this document sits in a bordered block below the frame, outside the bezel, in this document's voice. The owner can cover the markers with a hand and judge the product.

The one decisionGrants are drawn as resolved shapes and edited as clauses.

The other four proposals will hand the owner a grant as text and leave them to simulate its consequence in their head; this one makes the simulation the artifact, so the question what does this admit gets answered by an area before anything is committed. And it refuses to let the owner type into that picture, because the moment the drawing becomes the input, the overlapping-glob problem that got the grant matrix banned in the first place walks straight back in.

ClosingWhat this style is bad at.

  1. It dies on a phone. Two axes need width and there is none, so the field becomes a stacked list and the whole idea contributes nothing to jobs 1, 4, 5 and 7 on the surface where job 4 actually happens. Judge the phone screens against the other four proposals and expect this one to come third.
  2. It holds together only while the namespace set is small and sorts well. Seven rows read at a glance. Forty rows is a scroll, and the day the owner writes to personal:travel the sort that made project:* a clean rectangle starts fragmenting. The design leans on a property of the store that the owner can break by accident.
  3. Job 6 is worse here than in a triage inbox. Knowing a contradiction sits in user:me at open helps once. It says nothing about which side wins, which is the entire decision, and a proposal built around the queue will move faster through thirty items than this one moves through three.
  4. It is the most expensive of the five. The two screens that justify the style, reach and lens, are ledger items 2 and 3, and item 3 is the largest non-migration item in the document. Build this without them and the owner gets an outline with no numbers in it, which is a diagram of a promise.
  5. The second visit is thinner than the first. Inventory moves slowly. The week deltas are the honest answer and they are small numbers, so on most Mondays the map looks like last Monday and the arrivals rail carries the visit on its own.
  6. The map is a skill. It takes one explanation, and this document spent a whole section giving it. An interface that needs an explanation has already lost an argument to a list, and the owner should hold that against this proposal.

About the data on these screens

Every fact shown is invented, written against this project's real history and this repository's real vocabulary. The counts describe a plausible store of 1,240 live rows and 63 retired ones; nobody has measured the owner's store, so read them as a shape rather than a figure. Client counts are not padded. Two clients hold a credential in this story, claude-code-mac and chatgpt, plus the console's own row. openwebui, hermes and claude-ai appear nowhere on a screen, because they have not registered.

The 22 arrival rows are filler in the sense that floor 1 needed twenty and the invented week supplied twenty-two. The conflict is the real one from this project's history: the port fact in user:me, near-identical wording, which is the case supersession exists for.

Frames render at the viewport printed in their caption and scale down to fit this page. Every size quoted in the floors table is the native one.
