lumberroom notebook

Notebook

The store is one document. Reading it, correcting it and checking who else may read it happen on the same page, in the same rendering, so the owner learns one way of looking and uses it everywhere.

Every fact is an entry: a dateline in the left margin, the fact itself set in a serif at reading size, the client and the sensitivity in the right margin on the same line. A correction is a revision struck in place rather than a row that vanished. A client's grant is the readership of the document, written as a sentence before it is written as clauses. Triage is the margin of the page, where two entries that disagree sit side by side.

The owner asked for somewhere to look at their memories. This style takes that literally and then insists that looking and acting are one motion.

The apparatus

Anything on these screens that the server cannot answer today carries a small red reference, keyed to a note under the frame naming the ledger item it rides on. The references and the notes belong to this submission and never to the product, so the button in the top right removes every one of them and leaves the console the owner would use. Press it before judging the density.

What happens on a phone

Jobs 1, 4, 5 and 7 work end to end and the layout stops being a document with margins. The dateline moves above the fact, the client and namespace move below it, and the sensitivity word stays on the top line with the date. Fact text drops from 17px to 16px and the measure narrows to the screen, which holds it under 45 characters a line. Eight entries fit above the fold.

The margin is readable and carries no verdicts. Readership and admitting do not exist there. A fat-fingered ceiling change is the mistake this system exists to prevent and it is never urgent, so the phone cannot make one.

What this style is bad at

Triage at volume, and it is not close

A conflict rendered as a page spread with both provenances is the best reading of a disagreement any of these five styles will produce, and it costs 260 vertical pixels. Three items fill a screen. Thirty items is eight screens of scrolling and about four minutes of paging before the first verdict, where a dense queue would have shown all thirty at once. Nobody has counted the arrival rate, and §9 Q6 leaves it open. If it turns out to be five items a week this style is right. If it is thirty, the margin page needs a compact mode that undoes most of what makes it good, and the owner should pick the triage inbox instead.

Grant editing reads better than it edits

The sentence at the top of a readership page is the clearest statement of a grant in this submission, and it is read-only. Editing happens in the clause list underneath, which is an ordinary form wearing a serif heading. This style adds nothing to that work. It also cannot show two clients side by side, because a document has one readership at a time, so comparing what chatgpt sees against what claude-code-mac sees means two visits and a memory.

The document metaphor breaks at 1,240 entries

A notebook implies you could read it end to end. This one holds seven days and stops, because unbounded browsing belongs to the Obsidian mirror and §8 keeps it out of the console. A reader who takes the metaphor at its word will look for a way to keep turning pages and find a filter instead. The honest fix is the mirror, §6.2 item 11, one route and a CLI rewrite.

Whitespace is a bet

Twenty-one entries above the fold clears the floor and beats nothing else on the screen. A design that packed the same entries at 13px would fit forty. If the owner turns out to want density more than comfort, this style has spent its budget in the wrong place and cannot recover it without becoming one of the other four.

One ink, one paper

No dark theme is drawn. The whole hierarchy rests on families and sizes rather than on hue, which makes a dark rendering straightforward and untested. Sensitivity carries no colour at all, which is deliberate under floor 4 and does mean a private entry announces itself less loudly than a coloured chip would.

The decision this proposal would defend against the other four

Every screen renders the same object the same way. An entry on the arrivals page, an entry in a search band, an entry withheld under a lens, an entry in a conflict pair and an entry on the phone are one component with one typography, so the owner learns to read a fact once and never re-learns it. The cost is that triage and grant editing inherit a reading layout instead of getting purpose-built ones, and that cost is real and paid at volume. The return is that the console has no second visual language to teach, which is where the rejected preview spent the owner's attention and lost it.
